Methodology & caveats

How each number on this page was computed, what we sampled, and what we did not.

Source
One Excel workbook with five sheets, produced by JetHQ researchers and Supernal engineers during the period under review.
Validation rounds
Round 1 (Jan): smallest, earliest sample (505 rows in sheet, 74 reviewed). Round 2 (Feb–Mar): broader sample (8,234 rows in sheet, 53 reviewed). Update Validations (Mar–May): most recent full Marcus output (5,945 rows in sheet, 127 reviewed). Final List: curated, post-QC list of 127 reviewed items, re-reviewed by Supernal. This is the 99.21% headline scorecard.
Verdict definitions
Correct — Marcus classified the alert and applied the right update. Half Correct — Marcus picked the right classification but a downstream value (price, status, time) was off. Incorrect — Marcus's update was wrong.
Strict vs. weighted
Strict accuracy counts Half Correct as wrong. Weighted accuracy credits Half Correct as 0.5. The headline 99.21% is strict.
Who reviewed each round
JetHQ researchers raised flags. Supernal (Jens) re-reviewed every flag and produced the Final List of 127 verdicts.
How the years were inferred
Times in the workbook are bare HH:MM DD Mon strings. We inferred the year from Jens' notes that reference dated events in this calendar year and from data continuity across rounds. All times are treated as the same calendar year.
Data quirks we cleaned
Stray Good/Ok/Poor/Score values in the New Market Status column and numeric leakage in the Validation column (single-digit counts, decimal fractions, status strings) were filtered out. Only the four valid verdict strings are counted.
Sampling bias
The validated samples are not random — they are the rows researchers chose to flag. Reported per-classification accuracy reflects flagged outcomes, not Marcus's overall behavior on that classification. Classifications with fewer than five validated samples are marked low-sample.
What 99.21% does and does not mean
It means: of 127 updates that went through the full QC loop (researcher flag → Supernal review), 126 were Correct. It does not claim that Marcus is 99.21% accurate on every alert it processes — for the broader 5,945-row recent window, only ~2% was hand-validated.
